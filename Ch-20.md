<h3>Chapter Twenty</h3>
<h3>Bearly strong enough</h3>
</center>

The creatures were escaping. They floated at the speed of a sedate jog, in the direction of Tucson. 

"They are going toward Tucson!" I alerted everyone. 

"Frank! get the gun and more shells! Everyone else get your horses and follow!" McCoy shouted. "Emma, with me!" 

The area became a flurry of activity. People were scrambling about, while Decker ran to his Iron Steed. I followed, no time for arguments. 

"Jump in! I'm going to drive, you'll be tracking the creatures. How far are they?" he urged. 

As quickly as possible I sat in the little cart that was attached to the Iron Steed's side. I was in, but my skirt snagged on one of the many parts sticking out and tore down the side. 

_Wrong attire for this kind of work. No more skirts on adventures!_ 

McCoy kicked at the machine and it started its warbling. We tore off after the creatures. We had to avoid solid objects in the way, which they ignored like bad air. Trees, rocks, bushes, all of it a minor but compounding detour. Luckily, they weren't very fast and we caught up really quickly. Unluckily, we had no way of stopping them. 

Soon enough hoofbeats started catching up to us, with Frank passing me the gun and a box of shells, while the others rode alongside us. Mr. Song behind one of the agents, holding on for dear life. 

Decker turned to me and shouted in my face. "Watch the monsters! We need to get ahead! Cliffs!" He pushed the speed and veered off to the left. We rode downhill over rocks, roots and uneven terrain, dodging trees, bushes and low hanging branches. 

I was Witnessing and have been for a long time today. The brightness was being exacerbated by the warbling, while the shaking made me feel sick. I closed my eyes for just a moment and lost sight of the creatures. I searched the landscape in panic, when they emerged from the cliff wall we just didn't fall off of. 

I pointed to a small hill I thought the monsters would fly over and shouted. "That hill! We can make it!" 

We did make it, with an abrupt stop. It was too much, vertigo hit me like a runaway coach. I bent over the side and sent my lunch to fertilize the nearest patch of grass. I flopped out of the sidecar, managing to avoid the mess, but tearing my skirt even further.

"Emma, you need to be ready to shoot. Don't be scared, you'll love it." He disembarked and immediately started doing another of his dances. 

I almost jumped out of my skin when I heard a bear's roar behind me and felt a huge, warm presence against my back. Thick, semi-invisible golden bear arms settled along the outside of mine, following my movements perfectly. The world stopped spinning.

It felt amazing, the bear's strength adding to mine and making the massive gun feel no heavier than a broom. I could easily kick in a door or bite through a table. 

_You were right, I do love it._ 

I made sure the gun was loaded and took in the situation. 

Creatures ahead, floating toward us, not very high up. A hundred feet distance. 

ASI riders, far to our right, galloping at breakneck pace. 

Eighty feet. Hoofbeats.  
Forty feet. _Please make it in time._  
Thirty. Aim.  
Twenty. Click-clack-cht-cht-clack!  
___B'TOOM!___  
Flurry of gunshots.  
Impossible shapes.  
Reload.  
___B'TOOM!___  
Reload.  
___B'TOOM!___  
Reload.  
___B'TOOM!___  

And then, silence. A ringing kind of silence. A bear whine. My strength was my own now.

I was inhumanly tired, my arms and right shoulder complaining especially loudly. My eyes burned, my mouth tasted acrid and my hair smelled of burnt gunpowder. I was showing thigh and knee to the whole world, but I couldn't muster any care.

I sat down a few steps away, exhausted. Then I laid down, because it required less effort. I turned my head to feel the grass on my cheek. It was over. 

No it wasn't. I had turned my head toward where we came from. Six more creatures approached, at a speed far exceeding that of those before. I had no strength left. I was completely spent. I accepted my fate. 

You Bastard crashed into me and nuzzled, while the other five stood guard. 

"BWAAK! Boot boot prr BWAAK!" 

"Yes, I'm sorry I made you worry. I'm ok... It's fine..." I said weakly, patting his head. 

A shadow loomed over me as Frank extended a hand to lift me up. "You did good, Emma," he said as he pulled. 

A series of tired men went past. 

Pat on the shoulder. 

"Well done." 

"I'd ride with you again." 

The Nod. 

That made me proud. Really proud. These were compliments vastly different than to what I was used to. Earning the respect of these men carried weight, simply because of what they did for a living. 

_I don't think I will ever care as much for a "Nice dress" as I used to._
