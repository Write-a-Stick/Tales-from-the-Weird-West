<h3>Chapter Twenty-Five</h3>
<h3>Direction of secrets</h3>
</center>

I had things to do. 

I got up from bed and got ready to leave for the day. Normal things like breakfast, bath and grooming. I put on my best skirt, emerald green with a white top trim, to match the emerald green jacket with white cuff bands and a frilled white shirt. 

I remembered how I got them. I looked in the tailor's display once and immediately fell in love. But money was tight at the time, and while it was the right thing, I was still disappointed. But Dad remembered, and bought it once The Principal bounced back. _Thank you, Father Mine._ 

I took The Adventure Bag with me, now also occupied by You Bastard and the letters. I gave myself one last look in the hallway mirror before heading out to the bank. 

"Going on a date, Emma?" Tiffany asked, coming out from her room. "You look fancy." She smiled at me. 

"Oh, thank you. Actually, going to the bank, then ASI. It's been a wild few days." I shook my head and smiled back. 

"Yes, I heard a bit from Travis. You have to tell me about the monsters later. I don't want to have to wait for the book to come out." 

"I will." I waved at her and left for the bank. 

It was surprisingly quick and mundane. I was half expecting the bank teller to laugh at me, especially after the glances the guard and greeter gave me. But no, he read the note, stamped it and gave me a wad of banknotes and a few coins in return. 

"One hundred and thirty-eight dollars and thirty cents, Miss. Would you like to open an account with us?" the clerk asked. 

"Perhaps later, I have to meet someone first. Thank you, have a good day." 

I made a stop at home, to put away the money, It wasn't sensible to walk around with it. Next destination, ASI. 

After having been around ASI agents for a while, the previously seemingly suspicious, bland building felt different. Especially that I knew today's front table guards by name.

"Good morning, Bob, Harry." I waved at them. 

"Morning, Redcoat," Bob responded. _Redcoat?_ 

"My name is Emma, you know that," I objected to the nickname. 

"Not to us, it isn't." He grinned. "In you come, we were told to expect you." Bob got up and lead me inside. 

The first room was some sort of reception, with Neil and Clark manning the desks, writing something. Bob led me down a corridor at the rear of the room, upstairs, past closed and open doors. There was a plaque next to each. 

Archives.
Analytics.
Financial.

Finally we went into a sort of smaller, better appointed reception, the door labelled "The Director" at both the outer and inner doors. There was a desk and a few armchairs in the room, brightened up by the east-facing window and potted plant. 

Behind the desk sat a prim and proper lady, around thirty, her lips twisted disapprovingly as if she ate a lemon and chased it with vinegar. 

"Miss Coates is here to see The Director," Bob said and immediately dissolved into thin air. 

The woman scowled at me and pointed to the armchairs. Her heavy makeup was giving her an even sterner, commanding look. "Sit down, Miss Coates. The Director will be free to see you soon." 

I sat down and avoided looking at the woman, she felt too familiar, like that one teacher we all wished we never had. After a few minutes the door opened and Frank came out, quickly dashing past She-Cerberus and me, without looking. 

_She is one scary woman._

She got up and went into the office proper. After a moment she exited. "The Director will see you now, Miss Coates. Shut the door behind you." 

I got up, knocked and went in. It was a small, dark, windowless room, with a wooden desk and two armchairs. The man sitting opposite was dressed in a black suit, impeccably aligned and composed. The papers and writing utensils on the desk were arranged with obsessive attention to angles, lengths and distances. It was all I could see in the dim light of the downward facing desk lamp. His face was hidden in the shade, his movements reserved and calculated. 

"Sit, please, Miss Coates. I am The Director." He had a deep, calm, self-assured voice and a slow, deliberate cadence of someone who knows the ending of a conversation before it begins. "Thank you for coming." 

I sat down, somewhat uneasy. 

_Breathe. You know why you're here. You're ready._ 

"I got the note, the bank said you're good for the money. Why exactly am I here?" I asked. I had an idea, but I wanted to try and make him tell me himself. 

"I would like you to work for us, Miss Coates. The men tell me you have useful abilities. Abilities I need," he said and steepled his hands. "We pay well, as you know." 

"I do. I'm not going to lie, I'm interested. I never felt so horrifyingly alive and never made this kind of money before. Although, there are things I am unwilling to do." 

"I'm going to need to know what those are and if it's going to be a problem," he said. 

"I absolutely refuse to be made to do something morally objectionable. Killing, extortion. Normal bad things. Or helping bad things happen." 

He made a hand motion that suggested he considers the topic not worth discussing further. "Anything else?" 

"Yes. I refuse to be kept in the dark. I need to know, and I'm also going to need to know who I'm working for. I have developed a severe dislike of secrets. If we can agree on that, we should talk money." 

"The identity of The Director is revealed to all associates and agents, as a sign of trust. As far as secrets go, there will be many. Secrecy saves lives, and I am not willing to compromise on that. Operational manuals, the Bestiary and further training would be provided to you. We believe knowledge is power." _Knowledge is, in fact, power..._

"This is fair. I don't want to endanger people unnecessarily, if that's how it works. But I won't accept being not told _Just because_." 

He was silent for a moment. "I accept. The pay would vary, depending on our needs. No less than ten dollars for routine daily work, fifty for a high intensity day, like the one you had yesterday. Food, lodging, travel, medical and operational costs reimbursed, within reason." 

"You must be crazy. No one pays that kind of money—" I stopped myself. "I am sorry. It's just hard to believe there's no hook." 

"There is a hook. You never speak of any ASI business to any third party. Ever. Endangering our people is not something ASI can forgive." 

_I see. At least you're upfront about it. Harsh, but understandable. I need to be sure it's what I think it is._ 

"And what do you do if there's a witness to what you're doing? They never agreed to this and don't stand to get paid either." I couldn't read his face, as it was hidden in the darkness, but I could focus on his voice. There was no guarantee, but not trying would be negligent. 

"That's always a problem. First, we ask nicely. Then we offer money. Operational procedure allows for ominous looming and asking nicely again. If someone is particularly stubborn, we retell their story in the whole county. Ridiculous, obnoxious but entertaining lies. We simply make the facts uninteresting by comparison. Mr. McCoy seems particularly adept at that." 

I couldn't tell if he was lying. But the method sounded plausible and felt relatively benign. _I'll still be watching you._ 

"I agree." I extended a hand over the desk. 

"Welcome to the fold, Special Agent Redcoat." The man across took it and turned the lamp to illuminate his face. "Special Agent Clint Westwood, it's a pleasure," McCoy said, grinning. 

_No, you didn't just..._
