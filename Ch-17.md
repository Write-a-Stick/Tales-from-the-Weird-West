<h3>Chapter Seventeen</h3>
<h3>Deceptively simple</h3>
</center>

<center>----------</center><br>

'Can someone explain this to me?' A tired voice asked from the slowly rising heap of men. Tom Brine.

'I believe Mr. You Bastard's dawn-song staves away the illness. Whatever this illness is.' Song calling it a song was funny in that terrible way. 'I suggest you do not untie me. It is not gone.'

All eyes were now focused on You Bastard, who immediately puffed up and looked like he owned this big chicken coop with no chickens in it.

'Are you telling me that a singular chicken is more useful than the six of us? Also, is it just this one, or can we use others?' Tom turned to one of the other men. 'Frank, go buy us a couple roosters.' He then spoke to Dr. Endaway. 'Are we doing this here? Or are we moving out?'

'We should move.' Endaway responded. 'Miss Coates, can we take Mr. Peckersley with us for a time?' He asked me in turn.

I ignored the snickers coming from the ASI men.

'Bock Cadaa. Pock BWAAK!'

'He says no, bad feeling.' I translated for the benefit of the others. 'He also refused to leave my side since this morning. He may know something we don't.'

Now everyone was looking at me, tilted heads and slow blinking. Tom Brine broke the silence.

'Miss Coates, we need you to come with us. Please bring the chicken. We are going to need you too, Doctor.'

'As soon as possible, but I need to attend to Mr...'

'Song. Tie Song. Iron Pine in your language. Please, my arm is sore.' Only now I noticed he was serene through what must have been incredible pain. I did break my arm once, and just touching it made me cry. He was pretty much hog-tied. Previously I assumed he was just uncomfortable in the heap and making a flowery complaint. 

I also realized that these six men broke his arm without a second thought. That the pleasant Tom Brine orders ASI around, but gets ordered around himself by McCoy.

_I should have suspected more layers to this with McCoy regularly around. I knew, yet I ignored it. __Veni, non-vidi, victis__ Maybe. I came, I didn't see and lost. But I see you now._

'Alright, let's get in there then.' He motioned toward the nearest door.

'My office please.' Dr. Endaway redirected them. It was a shame I wouldn't be able to peek at his journal anymore.

They carried him in and I followed, naturally. Mr. Song was put on a chair and partially untied, to allow the doctor to work. It did not escape my attention that the ASI men quietly drew their weapons and hid them under their jackets, or with their other hands. The revolvers looked normal, if a bit large. Not that I was an expert. I did notice they were of the same make and well maintained.

'Are you ready, Mr. Song? I need to set it.' Dr. Endaway asked while holding the broken arm of his patient.

'Yes.' 

Crunch.

'Filthy son of a bow-legged duck!' Mr. Song shouted. 'Apologies. That hurt.'

'It's alright, Mr. Song, I've been called worse.' With that, he proceeded to dip bandage-like pieces of cloth in water, then wrap them around the patient's arm. The whole thing started to look like it belonged on a construction site, not a doctor's office.

'Mr. Song, do not move your arm too much for the next two weeks, the bones need to grow back together. The cast should keep your broken bone safe.' He took some powder from a pouch and sprinkled it heavily on the wet bandages. By some miracle of magic or science, the cast dried almost immediately and white granules fell off it to the floor.

'Alright everyone, let's go.' Tom said with a stern tone.

_No. You don't get to order me around._

'That's going to be a no from me.' I told him.

'Miss Coates, this is important. You need to come with us.'

'I'm not going. But where is it that you want to take me?'

'Somewhere where we can keep Mr. Song safe and see what your part in all this is. And his.' He pointed at You Bastard.

'Still a no. Stop being vague and I'll consider it.' I stood my ground.

'Fine. I didn't want to do this, but you leave me no choice.' He took a step toward me and looked me in the eyes. 'Miss Coates, we have a problem. A problem that you seem to have a solution for, and we need it badly. In the last few days, there have been at least five people affected by what you saw. And already not everyone made it home. Look me in the eye and tell me your time is more important than the life of another? Tell me this and I will leave you alone.' He smoothly went from threat to an elaborate moral quandary, circumventing any real explanation.

_You absolute bastard. You win this time._

'Fine, but tell me everything.' I started toward the door.

'I do not have the authority to tell you everything.' At least he had the decency to look apologetic.

'Then who can?' 

'I can't tell you.' 

We all entered the wagon waiting outside, Mr. Song on a stretcher. Some of the space was already occupied by Frank and three roosters in cages. Apart from the benches, there was a gun rack on the wall. It hosted a half a dozen long arms, gleaming and ready to use.

_That's a lot of guns._

'Then you're hardly capable of holding up your end of the bargain. What CAN you tell me?'

'Since you are a person of interest, I can tell you we're going to our field laboratory outside of town. We will be testing out, and I dread to say it, chicken solutions. Try to see what works and if it's permanent.'

Mr. Song, silent so far, asked his own question.

'This makes me believe I don't want to be there, in this field laboratory of yours.'

'Please don't be alarmed, the worst I can imagine us doing is trying the rooster's crows on you from a variety of distances. You are a victim here, not the culprit. But we must know.'

I didn't like the explanation. It sounded too close to 'The end justifies the means'.

'So you're going to do whatever you like to him and me just so you can know?' I fired at Tom. 'For the greater good?'

'Miss Coates, we are bound by a strict code of ethics. We absolutely _DO NOT_ involve the innocent and bystanders. Unfortunately, you are involved and therefore not just bystanders. But understand this: any sacrifice you make will be your decision. Yours alone.'

'What do you mean by "sacrifice"?'

He thought for a moment, letting the sound of wheels on cobbles fill the space.

'Hypothetically, would you choose to lose a finger so that someone you love could live?'

My thoughts immediately went to Dad.

'Yes.'

'How about an arm? Or leg?' He pressed.

'Yes.' I felt my confidence draining a little. He was leading me somewhere, and I really didn't like the moral shape of it.

'How about both legs and both arms?'

'Still yes...'

'Now how about someone you don't know? Or don't like? How about a city full?'

'I don't know! There's too much to consider!'

Tom smiled.

'I know. And yet, sometimes we are the only ones who see the decision has to be made. Would you prefer we just let everyone die, just so our consciences can be clean? We are aware of it and it is our sacrifice. To make those decisions, and live with the consequences. So go ahead, take the high ground, continue your snarky commentary.'

I hated the fact that he made a good point, one I couldn't easily refute. I tried another angle.

'So, why does Mr. McCoy order you around?' I watched him like a hawk. He thought for a moment, undoubtedly deciding what to tell me.

'Mr. McCoy is what we call a "Unique Asset". He knows we currently have no one to replace him, so he charges exorbitant rates and is pretty much given free rein. He is infuriating, but unbelievably effective.' Perfectly sensible explanation. 

_That's McCoy we're talking about. Give me more, Tom!_

'So if my assessment is correct, it would be catastrophic if he threatened to quit?' I risked using the specific words I read in Dr. Endaway's field journal. Tom held his face still, but I did catch a flash of concern mixed with uncertainty.

_You don't know if I know or if it's a coincidence._

'That would certainly make our work more difficult and probably more dangerous. But not impossible.'

_Stop evading!_

'What if the McCoy train went off the rails? Went completely insane?' I pushed.

'We do not discuss any potential contingencies we may or may not have toward any given individual or company.' Smooth, snappy, elaborate enough wording to be a concealed threat in and of itself.

'I see.' I had enough to think about for now. I saw the shape of the puzzle pieces, but I still couldn't see the picture.

_I really hope I'm not wrong about these people._
