<h3>Chapter Twenty-One</h3> 
<h3>Plan after it's done</h3> 
</center> 

We returned to the field laboratory at a comfortable pace, no one was in a rush anymore. The four victims were still unconscious when we arrived, Dr. Endaway attending them. Some agents apparently remained behind to protect it. I had thought everyone left with us, but on review, this was smarter. 

"Is the threat neutralized?" the doctor asked as soon as we sat down. 

"Yes," I confirmed. I did see all of the creatures get obliterated. 

"No," Decker said at the same time. 

The doctor and I looked at McCoy. 

"Why?" I asked, tired. I just wanted it to be over. 

"They came from somewhere. Events like this don't happen without someone making them happen," he explained.

"They don't? Right, so how do we find that someone?" 

Decker thought for a moment. "I don't know. And there are two other important matters we need to discuss. Are there more of the monsters in town and how do we kill them without frightening people? We clearly can't go in guns blazing on Main Street." 

Dr. Endaway thought for a moment. "Miss Coates, can you make guns quiet, like you did with the roosters?" 

It was my time to consider. "No, I'm not sure why that even happened, exactly. I really want that in my pocket, but I haven't figured it out," I admitted.

Someone brought us coffee and some food. To my shame, I was that girl. You know, that person who orders a salad and eats half of the big sharing platter. I couldn't help it, lunch was long gone and I was ravenous. 

When I noticed people looking, I stopped abruptly, mouth full, like a startled cross between a deer and a chipmunk. My cheeks probably turned the color of my hair. 

"—m hngy!" I covered my mouth and started chewing furiously, so I could speak a language other than Eatish. 

"Easy, don't choke." Decker patted my back. "No shame here, we know what that feels like." _Yeah, I guess you do, Mr. Crusty Wanderer._

With the crisis averted, the serious conversation continued. 

"We could use enquieteners for guns, but not for Wraithbreaker. That thing is just too big and too loud. Though if we stuck together, I know Travis could do it," McCoy suggested. "We'd be limited to one place and who knows how many of the things there are... We need to know how long it takes for someone to go crazy, too."

"I could help with that." Mr. Song offered, until now quietly sitting to the side and drinking tea. All eyes focused on him. "Do you know the thoughts that sometimes come without reason? Those that tell you to put your hand in a fire, kick a puppy or jump off a cliff? The thoughts no sane person ever entertains and pushes them back, because it's wrong to do?" 

General nods all around, myself included. 

"Think of the kind of effort it takes to not do. Not a lot, but you feel it. I think those creatures eat this effort. For the past ten days or so, I had trouble fighting those thoughts. More and more each day, until the... unfortunate incident from this morning." He shook his head in shame. 

Tom Brine laughed at it. "Sorry. I'm laughing now, but seeing Mr. Song punching bricks out from a building, one by one, is no laughing matter." 

I looked at Mr. Song in a new light. I did see him dodge the falling creature, but punching bricks out was ridiculous. "Tom, how the heck did you manage to tie up someone who can punch bricks out of walls?" 

"They shot me. A few times," Mr. Song interjected calmly. "My body still hurts." 

Seeing my confusion, Tom drew his chunky revolver, opened it with a flick and took a bullet out to show. "Punch Bullets. They make bruises rather than holes. Standard ASI front Six-Load." _Like a gun shooting batons... So much better than normal._ 

"So... is it like... doing something just because it comes to mind?" I asked. 

"Yes... but it's also like doing something you know you will regret, but you do it anyway." 

_Alright, Mr. Richardson... I understand. I do not forgive you, but I understand._

The situation wasn't looking too good. "We have one way of seeing the monsters, one way of maybe being quiet about it. What if there are too many?" I raised the problems. 

"Cabwaak? Boot boot prr dak dak bwaak," You Bastard said, drawing all eyes to me, somehow. 

"He says... uh... 'flock sing good'. Though I am unsure if he sees the scale of the problem. Saying that, neither do I. So far there is only one person who seems to know anything. Mr. Song, I think it's time you shared. Last time you mentioned Roosters." I looked at the old man. 

Unfazed, he met my eyes. "If I am right and you were born in the year of The Rooster... Sometimes, very rarely, a person gets born who has parts... No, aspects... Aspects of the mythical starsign." He started gesturing, suddenly animated. "A Rooster is about the boundary between day and night. Rebirth of light. Pushing back the darkness. Revelation." 

It was too vague for my liking. "You make me sound like I have an important role or I am some sort of saviour." 

"Are you not the single person who can reveal the monsters?" he retorted. 

"Well yes, I think so, for now... What about You Bastard? I think he can sense them too. How does he fit into this?" 

"Animal companion. Very traditional. More literal than is traditional. He must love you very much." 

"Bock bock. Boot boot prr." He bonked my knee with his head. He was adorable when he wasn't being his namesake.

_Stupid chicken. Boot boot prr to you too._ 

"Right, fine. Let's say I'm a Rooster. Hypothetically. Do I have a choice and is someone going to come after me? Speaking of, why have you?" 

"I had left my temple and went travelling, long time ago. I was close enough to feel your Awakening when it happened and wanted to meet you. This is not an everyday event, so I followed from Ohio. As for your other questions... You are what you are, but what you do with it is up to you. Certain people could come and try recruit you to their temples. Maybe." 

"Another thing I have to worry about?" I asked incredulous.

"No, not really, even if a temple noticed your Awakening. You are untrained, from a different culture and too old." _Well, now I'm kind of offended._ "The temples fight each other to prove the primacy of their style. Ancient rivalry. Very traditional. Very stupid." 

"That's all well and good," McCoy interrupted. "You can talk about Aspects, Roosters and temples later. Right now we need a solution." 

"I believe the ability is unique to Miss Coates and Mr. You Bastard," Mr. Song said. 

Dr. Endaway flipped a few pages in his field journal. "Evidence suggests this is the case. On a good note, Mr. Song's ten days give me hope. We have had only five cases in total. We can infer they were the first infected and reached uncontrolled state first. I estimate five or six more if the increase is linear, one per day. Though this number could prove to be flimsy reasoning." 

"Also, if the creatures remain hidden under a roof, I wouldn't be able to see them,"I said. "I could maybe feel them if I am close enough. Though... They did start going only after we killed the first one. It feels they are just as defensless against us, as we are against them. Like they were fleeing." 

"And they all were going in the same direction, weren't they?" McCoy asked. "That's fleeing toward safety, not from danger. We can track them to their source." 

"You don't know that, Decker," Dr. Endaway objected. "That's a big maybe, at best. We only know they run when detached and one is killed. We don't know what would happen to the victim if we shot one while attached, or if an attached one would even flee at all. Our best chance is to recreate the conditions from the laboratory." 

"Ok, so we need to make a rooster crow loud enough to be heard in the whole city. Then we intercept and blast them to hell. We leave one and follow it home. Then we blow it up." McCoy made it sound simple. 

And it was. Which absolutely didn't mean it would be easy. In the best kind of scenario, I still had to shoot that monster of a gun at least a half a dozen times. 

"And how exactly are you going to make him loud enough?" Endaway queried. _THAT is what you're worried about out of all the problems at our disposal?_

"I bought just the thing from a guy named Edison a year or so ago. Called it a mechaphone. Makes things louder. Much louder. We'll put it in the church steeple, next to the bell." 

Frank knocked on the table a few times. "You city boys clearly aren't aware that you can hear a regular rooster for miles, if it's quiet." That would put a stop to regular opposition. 

McCoy, clearly, wasn't regular opposition. "Sure, but Tucson is big. I'm not risking not getting all of them, in case one is on the outskirts. Also, I want a return on my investment. We're using the mechaphone." 

Planning continued into the evening, when everyone who could, went to rest. 

_Dear past Emma, someone should have told you adventure is what's left when you describe an experience so horrifying you need to scrub blood, guts and primal fear off every page._
