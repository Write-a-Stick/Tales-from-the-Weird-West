<h3>Chapter Twenty-Seven</h3>
<h3>For a breath of fresh air</h3>

I went outside and took in a chestful of air. The streets were mostly empty, lights illuminating them in circles. To my right, McCoy sprawled on a bench, legs extended. His clothes were slightly askew, which was a first since the day I met him. 

"If you were a customer, I'd tell you to go home." 

"Pff, make me." he mumbled. 

I sat down on the bench and without thinking I asked what was gnawing at me. "What happened to you back there? Who was Leonard?" 

Decker hesitated. "He's responsible for six people dying." 

"Did he..." 

"It's worse than that." He paused. "They were actors, kids not much older than you. He hired them to spread rumours and panic." 

Something was wrong. Not like Scry. Like... like I could feel a tear in his soul. I looked directly and it pulled. 

_Darkness, I am waiting behind bushes, watching the back door. The house was where the doomsayers were celebrating the end of preparations. Tomorrow was the day it was going to happen, by their own prophecies. Their prophecies of fire and lightning, earthquake and flood. Spreading the seeds of panic in and around Houston. I was ready to respond, but it was all an unknown. I had seen them come out transformed when exiting alleys, heard their wild-eyed speeches... They had to be stopped._ 

Flash. 

_I picked the door lock and slit the throat of the one in the kitchen, leaving him gargling on the floor. Corridor. Two more. Blood on the walls. I kicked the door into the main room and pierced the hearts of the nearest two. The third raised his hands to defend himself, a futile attempt to stop a full stab-through._ 

_Fear. No magic. No weapons. Just money and a primal fear of death. My sword clattering to the floor._

Flash. I was back.

_You... you did it... WE did it..._

My body froze from the crushing guilt, knees buckling under me. I deserved the pain. I didn't even try to protect my face as I fell. I should have checked better. Looked deeper. Asked more. Now, families won't see their sons, husbands and fathers. 

"Emma! What's going on?" McCoy rolled off the bench next to me. 

"I have to fix this. See their families, explain. Protect the innocent. Make sure everyone is taken care of," I told him darkly and pushed off the floor. 

"Emma, stop! It's done. It's not you, he made us do it and we paid the price, you and I. Paid everyone their due. In truth and in blood. Stop." 

Suddenly a few sober people ran out of Utopia, looking pale and unhealthy, but vertical and moving straight. 

There was clamour and hubbub, soon I was sitting up, watching Dr. Endaway do medical things to me and Decker. He gave me something to drink.
