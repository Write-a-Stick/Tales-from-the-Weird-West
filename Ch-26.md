<h3>Chapter Twenty-Six</h3>
<h3>The Tiffany Problem</h3>
</center>

I stormed out of that little office, just to see the reception filled with familiar faces. Tom, Frank, Neil and Clark. Even Tiffany, Bonnie and Travis. The sour receptionist was replaced by Hannah Ellison, the makeup completely changed. 

Frank took a step forward and handed me a lowball with an Atherton and a few ice cubes, just how I took it these days. "You with us, Red?" He smiled at me with those kind eyes. 

I felt their presence, and how they were all looking at me. Luckily, I knew how to see what was coming now. I quickly closed my eyes and turned away.

"A moment, please..." I said.

_Breathe. Again. Breathe._

I collected myself and faced everyone again. They all knew more than I did. At least until now. 

"You all have some serious explaining to do very soon." I downed the whisky. 

A wave of smiling faces and moving bodies pulled me toward the exit and into the street. Jim appeared from nowhere and started playing the fiddle, giving our twenty-something group of people a more urgent, faster cadence. As such things go, we reached Utopia quickly, Bonnie opening and closing behind us. Apparently, lock-in private party was the order of the day. 

Bonnie and Tiffany started pouring drinks, but soon abandoned the bar and let everyone help themselves. People split into smaller or larger groups, talking, drinking, dancing and playing cards. Everyone tried their best to move the last two days from "just now" to "back then". Sounded like a great idea to me. 

Toast. Atherton.  
"To Redcoat!" Everyone raised their glasses and I got a little embarrassed. And mildly annoyed at the nickname.  
"That's not my name! Also, I am American, born and bred and this name is ridiculous. It misses the point."  
Then they told me the other candidate names were "Bantam" and "Leghorn". _Red(hair) and Coat(es). Ugh. Fine, it could've been way worse. But you people need better ways to decompress._  
And now I had to think of a first name too.

Atherton, break in the din..  
"Someone please tell me the last two days weren't fabricated in any way," I demanded.  
Decker answered, as was right. He was the one ultimately responsible. "My identity obfuscation has a reason, but now isn't the time. The threat was real. We needed you and you answered the call. No more funny business, Jester's Honor." He definitely hadn't been pouring his drinks out.  
Tiffany came over and leaned herself on my shoulder. "Come on, little sister, we're sorry. Next time you're in on it though."  
_I am extremely unhappy that now it sounds like a great prank. Also, LITTLE sister?_  
"Tiffany, I'm eighteen," I said.  
"Yeah, exactly. I'm twenty." She said and I felt my jaw drop.  
"But... you look so... young," I stuttered.  
"Just because I don't have to take all measurements at the tailor, doesn't mean I'm not a woman!" She laughed, but I could see her glance at Tom Brine. 

Jim was playing a lively jig.  
Decker pulled Hannah into a dance. She was now wearing a perfectly fitting, silvery dress and moved with a flowing grace. _Woman has got style and poise. And money, to afford dresses like that._  
"Legend says they met on a mission, started comparing hidden arsenals. The rest is history," Frank said. I didn't see him approach, but he must have seen me staring.  
"Arsenals, huh? I believe that. Though I was mostly admiring her dress."  
"Oh, that. It's really nice, I heard Endaway say it's one of his best."  
Apparently the good doctor missed his calling, the tailoring was impeccable. 

Riesling and water.  
I was sitting at a large table, multiple conversations at the same time.  
"...she has that squinty look sometimes, have you noticed? Like in the cafe, she sent a pair of daggers my way..." I didn't exactly remember the man who stared at me when we first talked to Mr. Song.  
"Or at the lab." _Definitely about me._  
"You know I'm right here and can hear you? What's this about?"  
"You... have this look sometimes, pretty scary," Bob admitted.  
"Uh... sorry about that... It's just very intense. Too bright, too loud, too colorful. And people looking at me feels like..." I looked inside myself for a good comparison. "Like standing just a little bit too close to the fireplace. Not hot enough to burn, hot enough to want it to stop. Wait, why were you even there?"  
"We were making sure you were safe."  
I looked at Tiffany across the room. _You knew, and never once slipped up... Loyalty, or cunning?_ 

Afternoon. Lunch.  
The company, bleary-eyed and only collectively capable of standing, was eating Decker's Hands-On Stew.  
"Made with whatever I could get my hands on!" he shouted, ladling the brown and unappetizing slop into bowls.  
"Wonder what this one is going to be like." Someone said. Not sure who. But they sounded real hungry.  
"What the hell is in this?" I asked, devouring mine. "This has no right to be this good!"  
"Don't ask, we don't know, he don't remember." 

Decker and Travis on the stage, soothing flute and guitar, these two just playing, eyes closed.  
You Bastard making his way to greet Dr. Endaway, who came in alongside Billy from the ice cream cart.  
"Doctor, good afternoon, how is it you know You Bastard?" I walked up to him too.  
"Good afternoon." He rolled his eyes. "That would be Mr. McCoy. Brought me a rooster to examine one day, with 'Is this a normal chicken?'"  
"Is he?"  
"Medically speaking, yes. Though his full name on the form was a mouthful. McCoy named him that just because he knew I would abhor it, and hoped you would too. Do not give the man the satisfaction."  

Tom Brine in the corner. No Tiffany in sight.  
"B-word!" I pointed at him.  
"Uh? Oh, it's 'Boss'". 

Bonnie behind the bar, leaning in her favourite spot.  
"You've been watching Billy."  
"No, I— Yes. He's not ASI, right?"  
"Used to be. One day he got really unlucky, but some of the people here wouldn't be with us if not for him," she said.  
"And now he has to sell ice cream to make ends meet? I guess he's not on the street, which is good."  
She giggled, very ladylike. I stared. "Don't be silly. He gets paid until he dies. He can't sit still, so he sells ice cream and charges people on how nice they treat him."  
I remembered the sign. ```Ice Cream 1-10c.```  
It was unheard of. It wasn't how the normal world worked. _Correct. And also correct. But I'm not exactly in the normal world, am I?_ 

The revelries slowed to a glacial pace, everyone had more than their fair share and was in various states of regression.  
Someone gave beer to You Bastard, who now decided Frank's upturned hat was his roost and angrily repelled any attempts at being relocated.  _Bock cadaaa means bock cadaaa..._  
Frank himself was trying to convince the table he was lying on to let him go home.  
Various other bodies lying and sitting about displayed signs of heavily impaired decision making a few hours prior.  
The air became stale, so I went out for a fresh breath of night air. 

_It feels so light._
