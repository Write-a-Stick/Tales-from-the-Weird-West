<h3>Chapter Ten</h3>
<h3>What a lovely normal day</h3>
</center>

Not a day has passed and Tiffany's warning of expectations came true. They came true the next morning in the shape of Bonnie.

'Emma, I need you to help at the bar today.' These were simple words, but when said by Bonnie, mildly unsettling. I've seen her handle the whole thing herself with little trouble, so my imagination ran wild with how busy she expected it to be.

'Sure, you'll have to show me what's where and I'll need to get used to the layout, but I'm happy to help.' To be completely fair, I missed working with Dad and the normality of serving drinks felt like a nice change of pace. 

_I could do with normal._

'Pay is $2 per day.'

_Aaaaand it's gone._

That was daily pay for skilled labour, like blacksmiths or carpenters. As a woman, I would normally be shorted down to 60c from the $1.10 a man would get.

_Ok, not all normal is better. Fine._

'Opening at noon, like the board says?' Bonnie nodded. 'See you then.'

I spent the remainder of the morning getting ready. Normally it only takes a few minutes, but I couldn't resist a bath with hot water on tap. I still managed to be half an hour early, smelling fresh. I put on the crisp new black apron Bonnie handed me and had a proper look behind the bar.

Wine glasses at the back, beer glasses and steins under the bar. Whiskey in bottles, labelled intricately. Wine carafes to the side, ready to air the reds.I took a tumbler and pulled each of the four draught beers and tasted them. Decent flavours. Hoppy, malty, in-betweens. All of them cold. _Of course._

Then Bonnie showed me the ice machine.

'Watch the ice level in this little window here.' She showed me an ornate brass pipe with a vertical slit on it. 'Pull the lever, get a feel for it.' I did a full pull and five or six ice small ice blocks arranged themselves in the pipe with a rattle.

'Just how expensive is wasting ice around here?' I asked, just in case.

'Dirt cheap. We offer it as standard for any drink, no extra cost. Though if you start building a snowman, I AM going to charge you.'

I did a few more pulls to get the feel for getting less cubes. Not hard, I could feel the lever dispensing the cubes as I pulled.

Then I took a tray and bent and twisted it a bit. Smacked it on the bar. 

'Emma, why are you abusing my trays?' Bonnie wasn't happy.

'I need to know how they behave. I don't want to be going spilling drinks and breaking glasses all over.' She raised an eyebrow, but said nothing. 

I put glasses of water on one and tried it, single arm and both. They were solid wood. Rigid, pretty much unyielding. Exactly what you want from a good tray.

Ten minutes before opening Tiffany showed up and I noticed I was the only one wearing a skirt rather than trousers. She had her hair braided back, with a no-nonsense leather strap tying it together. Much less tomboyish than usual, but I still wouldn't have called it 'girly'. Apparently that wasn't required.

I wiped the tables and bar out of habit and went to open the doors. 

'How busy do you expect it to be today?' I asked Bonnie when I came back behind the bar.

'Should be pretty normal...' She looked outside for a moment and started pulling a beer.

'Regular?' Something we did for regulars at the Winchester, too.

'Mhm.' She nodded at the door. A young black man in a crisp suit entered. It was one of McCoy's friends, Jim. I couldn't remember his surname, always had trouble with French.

'Good afternoon ladies!' He greeted us with a smile and sat down in front of the beer and peanuts Bonnie set on the bar for him. He seemed much more approachable when I hadn't just been wrung out like a rag.

'Good afternoon Jim, door duty again?' Bonnie asked him.

'Yes, my turn.' He said it like it's a day off, smiling. 

'I don't get it. You were with Decker in Canton, and now you're here, keeping the peace in a pub. This seems comparatively... mundane.' I noticed in the corner of my eye that Tiffany straightened at those words.

'So? I'm McCoy's assistant. I do what I'm told. At least for the next four months.'

'Are we talking about the same McCoy? He doesn't seem the type to need or ask for much help?' I queried.

'Don't I know it. Not much, but when he does... OOF... Let's say boring and mundane is a fair bit better than the alternative.'

'So why four months? Do you have a contract of some sort?'

'You could say that. Still, could have been a hell of a lot worse. I get paid.' He smiled to himself.

At that moment customers started trickling in and that conversation had to be cut short. Just my luck.

'I'll talk to you later, Emma.' He took his drink and snack and relocated to the armchair next to the door.

We served the customers, pulling beers, pouring whiskeys and bringing out food from the kitchen. I got to meet Gary, the old grumpy cook. His food looked decent, but the man himself smoked like a chimney and swore like a sailor. His craft was nowhere near Dad's, but he was fast. Ridiculously so.

Utopia seemed pretty popular for lunch, despite the food being barely above average in quality. Another curiosity. I guessed it had to appeal to many tastes, since the clientele was wide and varied. 

_There it is. It's not the food. It's the people. Everyone being served the same, regardless of race._

I should have expected no less from a place that didn't apologize for what it was. It felt even more natural than at The Principal. It felt like betrayal, placing it second in anything. For the second time in the last weeks I pondered whether buildings could have feelings.

_I should probably ask about that..._

After the lunch rush was done and tables cleared, Bonnie called Tiffany and I behind the bar.

'I'm going out. You two are in charge. Jim will handle all that needs handled.' She announced.

'When are you back?' I asked.

'Tomorrow. I'm taking a day off.'

'Are you sure you don't want Travis to be around?' Tiffany asked.

'No. He said I'm too uptight, need to let go and take a day off. Don't disappoint me.' Then she left us with Jim at the door and a few customers that clearly had nothing better to do.

I turned to Tiffany.

'How do you want to do it?'

'I'll handle the regulars, tell you who's who and you take the rest?' She suggested.

'Sure, I can do that.'

The next few hours went by in relative peace, we washed the glasses and plates, dried and polished them. I started carrying a cloth tucked under my apron strap, it was easier than looking for one each time. 

It picked up in the late afternoon, maybe early evening. Tired men coming in after a long shift, in need of a few drinks before they go home to their wives. A story as old as time, though I wasn't exactly sure why.

Tiffany was familiar with about half the guests, knew them by name and drink. I could see Bonnie's School of Unshakeable Bartending plain as day. 

Once we closed around midnight, we headed back into the house. 

'First for a bath!' She claimed. I was severely disappointed in myself for not remembering that was an option. On the other hand it was a luxury I wasn't used to, after all. 

'I'll be quick, give me fifteen minutes?' That only sounded ridiculous if you didn't have hot water on tap.

I later laid in bed, refreshed after a long day.

_I could get used to this._
