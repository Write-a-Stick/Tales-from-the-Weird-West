The very next day I got a taste of what learning from Decker was going to be like. I wish I could say it started normal, but... well, McCoy.

'We no longer have the luxury of time. We start training today.' He told me as soon as he strutted into the kitchen in the morning.

'Hold on, please. What training? Why do we not have time? Time for what?' I refused to just be told.

'Well... nothing for you specifically to be worried about. But I can't always be around, and yesterday's encounter with Mr. Song showed we aren't always as safe as we think. I need you to be able to take care of yourself. Guns and swords. You'll love it.'

Tiffany put her face in her hands and sighed heavily.

'Ok, guns I can understand. But why fencing? I'm not rich enough to have it as a hobby and I have no intention of chasing trouble like you.' I asked.

'Because trouble may find you regardless and sometimes a gun just isn't enough. I’ll be damned if any apprentice of mine dies from anything less than a professional assassin.'

'You're joking! Also, I never agreed to being an apprentice!' I protested.

'I know things you need to know and there is no school to learn it at. That's what an apprentice is. _And_ I made a promise to your father.' I could see his point. But I was under no obligation to be happy about it.

'You will not leave my care without the ability and force of will needed to choose for yourself. You might not know it yet, but you have enemies. Or will. There are those who will try to use you, or kill you, because of what you can do.' It was a chilling revelation.  

In the corner of my eye, I noticed Tiffany stiffen at those words. She suddenly looked a lot more focused. 

'What if I say no?' I wasn't going to, he made his point. But I wanted to hear what he had to say.

'Then I will hire a half a dozen men to follow you around everywhere, whenever I think I need to. Like when I'm not in town.' He said with a smile.

'That would be a ridiculous cost! Assuming you take it seriously and not hire some drunkards.' I pushed back again.

'It would, but I do treat my promises seriously. Not to mention I'll get it back.' 

'And who on Earth would pay you that kind of money?' I laughed at the ridiculous notion.

His smile did that thing. Whatever was coming next, I wasn't going to like it.

'I know a guy. He'd give me the shirt off his back to pay it.'

_BASTARD!_

'Fine, you win.' I gave in. He probably wouldn't, but he could. And Dad did not deserve to pay the price, if McCoy would.

---

Right after breakfast he, Tiffany, Robbie and I took horses from the stable. Decker's horse, in addition to its rider's ego, was additionally burdened with an armful of rifles wrapped in a blanket. We went out of town to a secluded clearing slightly off the beaten road, where a firing range was located. Square wooden braces were set at various distances, thick metal plates hung off beams on ropes or leaned on supports.

We got off the horses, Robbie visibly eager for what was to come. Decker gave him and Tiffany a rifle each, though Robbie's was visibly smaller. They both turned the rifles in their hands, inspecting them with an unexpected level of proficiency.

'Weapon ready.' They said almost in unison. This felt like how I imagined military structure to look like.

'Get the paper targets set up, please.' He handed them some rolled sheets of paper and they left toward the wooden frames, rifles slung over their shoulders. He handed me one. 

'Have you ever used one of these?' He asked.  

This was the exact same model Dad owned, a Winchester Model 1873. I remembered some of the lessons he gave me in the past. 

'Dad showed me how to shoot his a few times. I can't say I'm very good at this.'

He went into the basics, like always assuming it's loaded, never pointing it at anything I didn't want dead.

'Never forget it's an instrument of death and that it wants to kill. Treat it with the respect it deserves. Go on, take the leftmost target, put some bullets in it.' He handed each of us a full box from his saddlebags.

I took my position, loaded the rifle, and started shooting at my target. It was a bit difficult to aim at first, with unexpected booms and cracks coming from Tiffany and Robbie’s weapons startling me at random. Decker corrected each of us, when he saw us do something incorrectly. I found myself making sure I was doing it right, even when I wasn’t the one being specifically addressed. 

'Tighter against your shoulder, Emma. You'll regret it if you don't.'

'Tiffany, open your other eye. Never lose track of your surroundings.'

'Don't yank the trigger. Slower. Pull.'

The most interesting guidance was the one he told our little man.

'Well done, Robbie. Very accurate, but slow. Sometimes more bullets in your target faster is what you need.'

'But Uncle Decker, if I miss, the bullet will be wasted.' Robbie argued. 

'No, Robbie. Not wasted. Missing is part of learning how to shoot better. I’d rather you miss a thousand times on the range than once when it really counts.'

Robbie thought on it for a while, then the cracks from his gun became much more frequent. Predictably, his accuracy suffered. Unpredictably, all his shots still hit. I decided to try it myself, sending my bullets toward the target.

_Well, I at least scared it._

Eventually the ammunition was gone and I had to admit I had a good time. The feeling of power and agency in my hands was exhilarating.

We returned to the house for lunch, but had to clean the rifles first. It took the better part of an hour to get them clean enough for Decker.

'Take care of your weapon, and it will take care of you.'

Another one to remember. The man was surprisingly insightful today. Though I did wish he sounded less like a proverb dispensary.

After lunch of beef stew and bread, courtesy of Travis, Decker decided it was time for fencing lessons. He took us to the fenced back garden. Robbie didn’t join us this time, but watched from the bench near the door. I guessed he was too young. 

Decker started the lesson, drawing his sword. I was watching, so I could see him grab a handle-looking thing from his belt. By the time he pointed it at me, it unfolded into that gleaming mechanical thing I saw when he made his oath to Dad.

_Of course he has a folding sword._

'It is a time honored tradition for the teacher to contemptuously obliterate any hopes the student might have had of their ability with a sword. Lucky for you, it's not a good use of our time. I will forgo my right as long as you take my instructions to heart. Watch.'

He stepped in front of a mannequin, drew his sword and cut three times. From the left, right and above. All three target sticks were neatly cut.

'Precision is key. You need to learn to guide your blade correctly. Align the blade with the swing. Otherwise you might as well use a club, it'll be better. Try it.' He handed me a sabre in a scabbard.

I stood in front of the other dummy, pulled the sabre out and took a practice swing. Not very heavy on its own, but once swung it was surprisingly hard to stop and guide. I tried hitting the sticks and predictably failed. Decker looked thoughtful.

'I see no bad habits, just a novice. Good. The second thing is posture and position. You need to be able to move quickly, know where to move and how to make your opponent follow.'

'That sounds like a very complicated set of dancing steps... How do you keep track of all this?'

'I don't. I don't have to think about edge alignment or foot position, I already know the deadly dance of steel. If I die by the sword, it won't be due to lack of skill, but contempt for my opponent. I'm going to trip on dogshit and split my head open on a rock. Or some other disgraceful death, unfit for a true hero.' 

'And I'm guessing it all takes practice?' 

'Yes, day in, day out, until your hands and feet bleed. But you don't need to get this good. All I need from you is to be able to fight and win against two unskilled fighters.'

'Two? At the same time? How long is it going to take?' Being able to do that sounded like quite the achievement.

'As long as it needs to, but you're smart, three months should do it.' He turned to Tiffany. 'Can you get us two practice swords Tiffany?'

He took one of the carved wooden swords, she kept the other. They stood in front of each other in what I assumed were fencing stances, all bent knees and arms in uncomfortable positions. They moved backward and forward for a moment, circling the center of the grass. It already looked like a dance, there was a move and a counter-move to everything. Decker connected his stick with Tiffany's and kept sideways pressure, tilting toward her.

She stepped back and let his stick pass, then smacked it from the other side with a wild, strong swing, trying to close the distance. McCoy quickly retreated, putting his sword between them, point forward.

Tiffany tried to smack it sideways, only for McCoy to bring it back with a flick of the wrist. A bit of a stalemate. 

I could see the difference now. He was taller and had longer arms, so she had to work twice as hard to even be able to attack. It was unfair and I let him know that.

'That's unfair. You have longer reach than she has. This says nothing about skill.'

'Does it not?' McCoy looked me in the eyes and grinned. Then he flipped the sword in his hand and passed it to me, grip first.

_Me and my big mouth._

'Let's see how you do against Tiffany.'

'I barely held a sword once in my hand, and it was today! Of course she is going to win!' I protested, but took the sword.

McCoy turned to Tiffany and said words that are going to haunt me for some time.

'Tiff...' I could see her brows knitting. _Daughter Mine, hah._ 'Emma is now your apprentice, too... And she thinks it looks easy. Why don't you show her who I have to deal with.'

I didn't like her grin, not one bit.

What followed was a thorough, contemptuous and painful series of proofs that Tiffany is, in fact, quite handy with a sword. Showed me how little I knew, I raised my hands in surrender.

'She could easily kick your ass, even if there were three of you.' He sure wasn't sugar coating it.

She looked at him with fury in her eyes.

'You. told. Me. I. Have. Much. To. Learn. Still.' She gritted.

'I did, I just failed to mention that you're better with a sword than most veteran cavalrymen I knew.' 

'I hate you sometimes. I really do.' She said, but she was smiling.

_I should probably get good quick, or get used to bruises._
