<h3>Chapter Nineteen</h3>
<h3>Science!</h3>
</center>

We started by setting up a big tent away from the rail cars and moving one of the unconscious victims there. Dr. Endaway's working theory was the monsters somehow made people aggressive, and a rooster's crow threw the creature back.

We were at it for hours. We tested everything, many times. I had read a few books about stars, mechanics and chemistry in the past and while I didn't understand most of their content, I knew of the scientific method. The doctor organized the whole thing according to principles like repeatability, challenging assumptions and preferred simplicity. I did realize that many things had to be checked many different ways, I just never quite realized how much work went into being able to produce one single fact.

100 feet.  
You Bastard. No effect.  
Another rooster. No effect.  
You Bastard and three roosters. No effect.  
You Bastard in sight, not crowing, while the others did. No effect.  
First You Bastard, then the others. No effect.  

Herding roosters. Lunch. The birds got bread. Frank returns with more birds. 
Senses return to normal, just the uneasiness left.
Hood back on. Look for the feeling. Bright and loud again.

50 feet.  
You Bastard. Creature wobbles in the air.  
Another rooster. No effect.  
You Bastard and three roosters. Creature wobbles more...  
You Bastard and five roosters. Creature detaches.  
Turn the roosters around. Creature detaches. Detached creature temporarily pushed away. 

75 feet.  
You Bastard. No effect.  
You Bastard and five other roosters. Creature wobbles.  
You Bastard at 75 and one rooster at 25. Creature wobbles.  

Again.  
Change. And again.  
And change.  
And again.  

"We have it!" Doctor Endaway roused me from the molasses river my thoughts were in.  

"We do?" _Finally!_ 

"Yes. And it was high time we pinned it down, I am unwilling to let the creatures reattach for the sake of research. I think we know enough to try," he said. "While each rooster seems to have a similar effect, only Mr. Peckersley's crow is strong enough to detach the creature on its own. But as long as the others can hear it, their crow also works within ten to fifteen feet." He took out one of his notebooks and wrote something in it. 

I looked at the creatures for the thousandth time, it was my job here. They were still ugly, but no longer shocking. I was feeling the strain, but thanks to the breaks and the hood, I could take it at least once more. I put on the hood. 

I looked inside myself for that feeling of non-descript wrongness. Scry, I called it, and by now I knew what to look for. If it was there, I could pull at it, and it would make me Witness. 

Witness was the more useful, but very uncomfortable part, especially if I was being stared at. 

"They are all hovering over the respective victims," I told him. "This all seems like temporary medicine, they look like they will try and reattach at some point." 

"I see. Probably the next step is to have you shoot at it, Miss Coates. How are you with a rifle?" He asked. 

"I trained a little bit. If I am close, hitting shouldn't be a problem. Which one should we go for? The smallest or largest?" 

"The largest is over Mr. Song." He turned to face the bound man, who until this point was just sitting there either observing or resting. "You seem the most resilient of all our victims and the only one conscious. I need to ask, for the greater good... Will you participate?" 

Mr. Song opened his eyes. "It doesn't seem like I have a choice, really," he said. "I do appreciate you asking, though. Yes, proceed." He closed his eyes again. 

Tom Brine decided to join in as well. "Thank you, Mr. Song. We appreciate your cooperation." He looked relieved. "My soul is gray enough as it is." _Huh... Ok Tiffany, I'm beginning to understand why you like him. The man has a proper spine._ 

I was given a loaded rifle shortly after. I took my time, steadied my breath and slowly went through all the checks I remembered. I took aim at the bloated creature over Mr. Song's head from fifteen feet. Impossible to miss, really. 

___BANG!___ 

It was inhumanly loud, much louder than at the range. Loud and unpleasant enough to make me jump. 

The effect was less than impressive. Which is to say, I either missed, which I doubted, or bullets couldn't hurt it. After a brief pause to make ear stoppers of beeswax, I shot it twice more. 

"I am sure I hit it all three times. Like it never happened," I reported. 

"That was silver, too," Tom said. "We could try a Wraithbreaker, but there's a problem. You wouldn't be able to handle it, and nobody else can see the monsters." 

"Why wouldn't I be able to handle it?" I asked. 

"I'll show you." He went into the right train car and returned with an obscenely large shotgun. 

The barrel was big and thick. The stock was heavy oak with a thick felt shoulderpad. Single shot, weighed a ton. Tom was right, It was too heavy for me to aim. 

"What is this thing anyway?" I got curious. 

"It shoots special platinum or gold slugs. Yes, it's ridiculously expensive to use. Five to twenty dollars per shot. Kicks like a mule." He preempted the obvious question. 

_They shoot gold at things. And platinum, whatever that is. Bang, a week's wages gone. Ridiculous, obscene, yet somehow I'm not even mad._ 

"Why gold though?" I asked. 

"The way Decker explained it was something about gold and platinum being very heavy and by that very virtue is _Obnoxiously there_. That somehow makes insubstantial things, like wraiths, substantial. Like an anchor. After that, well, you riddle it full of holes with silver. They just happen to be extremely expensive." 

_Ghosts and wraiths. Great._ 

"Why not a brace from the gunports?" the previously silent Frank asked. 

"Do it." Tom handed him the gun. 

Frank went into the right car, opened one of the windows and within five minutes mounted the massive shotgun on an articulated metal arm. In the meantime Doctor Endaway untied Mr. Song, who rubbed his wrists, looked up for a moment and moved closer. 

"I am ready when you are." Mr. Song stood about fifteen feet in front. 

A low, warbling noise reached my ears, coming from far away and getting louder by the second. Last time I heard it, it announced the arrival of McCoy. Soon enough, we all saw his ridiculous half-cape fluttering in the wind behind him, Corvus holding on the bar next to his hand. both were wearing scarves and goggles. 

Speak of the Devil and he shall appear. McCoy appeared when you blissfully managed to forget he existed. I wasn't sure which was worse. _Oh well, the Devil you know..._ 

"Lady and gentlemen." He nodded at us. "Do we have progress?" 

"You're suspiciously, conveniently, right on time for the test that seems most promising." I said. 

"A little birdie told me." He took a little notebook out of his pocket and waved it at me. I saw two similar notebooks like that. Both Tom Brine and Dr. Endaway wrote in theirs today. 

"Why am I even surprised? Also, can I have one once this is all over?" 

Dr. Endaway briefed him on the research findings, Tom on the plans to shoot the creature. 

"I need to see for myself," he said and made a little dance, like an extremely truncated variant of the dance I saw him do an the hill outside Canton. "I see nothing." He turned to me. "Emma, I'm going to need your help. I need to see through your eyes." 

"Uhhhh... How? And is this going to hurt?" I asked, slightly concerned. 

"I call it 'Passenger'. It's not painful, but extremely disorienting and sometimes unpleasant. But not painful. Mostly, it's going to feel like you're in two places at once, while someone is elbowing for room in your head. Fair warning, though. I will see what you see, including things you imagine. And PLEASE don't push me out forcefully, it's probably the second worst pain I ever experienced." 

"That does sound very unpleasant... Alright, let's do it." I agreed reluctantly. 

He sat us down in the center of the clearing, side by side on the ground. I put the hood on. He put his hand on my back and closed his eyes. For a while, I felt nothing, then the feeling came. Decker's description was pretty accurate. I felt the pressure, like standing shoulder to shoulder in a massive, pressing crowd, just in my head. 

_Hello, Emma. Love what you've done with the place._ 

I got startled by a voice delivering notes in a way reserved for me, myself and I. 

_Decker, this really IS unpleasant, let's get it over with quickly,_ I responded.

_Fair. I'm just a passenger here, the ability to see is all you. Whenever you're ready._ 

I focused on Scry, though the pressure of Passenger made it more difficult to focus. After a few seconds the now familiar brightness and elevated noise of Witness returned.

_Ouch. Oof. You're made of sterner stuff than I thought. This is nasty,_ he said.

_Mhm._

I looked toward the creatures. Saw all five in turn, in all their black, ugly glory. I could feel Decker's presence elbow itself closer to the eyes.

_These are definitely in the top one hundred disgusting things I have ever seen._

_I've been admiring them all morning. Haha. Are we done?_

_Yes, just a moment._

I felt the pressure shift, then lift. I was alone in my head again. _So much room to think!_

It was time. I went into the rail car and took position behind the mounted gun. 

Frank went in with me. "The train is unpowered, I'll have to charge it up manually for you." He pulled a lever a few times and a quiet whirring sound came from a metal bulb attached to the weapon. It immediately felt like the weapon was resisting being moved, but somehow lighter and more stable.

In the meantime, everyone else drew weapons and prepared themselves along the length of the car.

I aimed the Wraithbreaker at the monster over Mr. Song.

I waited for confirmation. 
I got it. 

___KABOOM!___

The sound was thunderous even with the earplugs. The weapon bucked painfully in my hands, even with the brace. 

The next few seconds were a flurry of activity. There was a noise between a screech and someone passing gas. Mr. Song dashed nimbly to the side to avoid the falling creature. As soon as it hit the ground, a hail of gunfire riddled its black body. I saw pieces of not-flesh being torn out and dissipating into impossible shapes. 

Four-sided triangles.  
A big nothing.  
Circles that weren't exactly round.  
Seven-sided cubes with parallel sides.  
A small infinity.  

"Cease fire!" someone ordered through the ringing in my ears.

Everyone gathered to admire the handiwork. Nothing was left apart from a not insignificant number of white and yellow metal particles where the creature used to be.

"Emma, is it dead, or gone?" McCoy asked.

"I think it's dead. I saw it dissipate," I confirmed. "Time to get the other four."

I looked in the direction of the other car.

_Fuck._
