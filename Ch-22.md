<h3>Chapter Twenty-Two</h3> 
<h3>The Roostering</h3> 
</center> 

Everything was as ready as it was going to be. McCoy's Iron Steed waiting in the street, the articulated brace from the train car welded in front of the side car seat. Travis, Tom, Frank and half a dozen other agents on horseback. Decker, You Bastard and me in the church steeple, waiting for dawn. You Bastard's crowing aid stood on three wooden legs next to the bell, facing east. 

The plan was simple. Wake up the whole city. Find the creatures and kill them. Follow the last one. So many things could go wrong. Especially the following part. I didn't like it, so much relied on me doing my part right. 

"It's almost dawn." McCoy broke me out of my reverie. "Get ready." 

Soon the horizon broke with the slightest sliver of the sun, blinding in its orange fury. The sun's flaming light reached You Bastard's eyes and gleamed off them. 

You Bastard inhaled and we put wax in our ears. 

"Cockadoo!..." He started, facing north. "Cockadoo!" West. "Cooo!..." South. 

The machine faced east, towards most of the city. A polished brass plaque gleamed the words "'Megaphone', Pat. pending" at me. 

_"Megaphone", Decker. Megaphone._

You Bastard took his biggest breath yet.

_Here we go_ 

___"CADOODLEDOOOOO!"___ 

The obnoxious, ridiculous, thunderous, vibrating crow washed over the city, echoed and repeated by a myriad of others, in unison. 

___"CADOODLEDOOOOO!"___ 

You Bastard stood there, both proud and stunned at himself. McCoy immediately blocked the machine's speaking apparatus and we removed the wax. 

I put on the borrowed hood, closed my eyes and looked into myself in search of that non-descript feeling of wrongness. It wasn't there. Scry wasn't there for me to pull at, I couldn't Witness. Wanting it really hard didn't work either. 

"Decker, I can't... the feeling isn't there," I said. 

"You could do it yesterday. What's wrong?" he asked. 

"Nothing! That's the problem. If I don't feel something's off, it doesn't work." I started getting desperate, I was the crucial link here and I was failing. 

"Right... what does it feel like? Does it make your skin crawl?" 

I thought about it quickly. "Not exactly, but close."

He pointed in the direction of a far away factory. "Focus on that chimney. See how it smokes? How the air makes it go into these little clouds?" 

I did as he asked. The chimney was there, but the smoke just did smoke things. It was normal. 

Suddenly, I felt my skin crawl, on my back and neck... It wasn't Scry... but it was really close, really odd. So I grabbed it. 

"Eeeeep!" I made the sound the moment I Witnessed. 

I turned around to see McCoy holding a feather from his hat about neck height. 

"Did it work?" he asked. 

"Yes, somehow," I replied and focused on the space beyond the steeple, over the city. 

I saw only three, hovering over roofs of houses, not at all close to each other. "There, there and... there." I pointed. "Also, we need to start moving, I see lots of people looking from windows and in the streets." 

"Right. Which one would be the easiest to shoot from the ground?" He asked. 

I pointed and described the slightly dirty red brick building to the west, a few streets away. 

"I know where we're going." He started running down the stairs. 

I grabbed You Bastard and followed, making sure not to trip and fall. _I really need to buy trousers._ 

While I was making big steps going down, using the bannister to turn sharply at each landing, McCoy seemed to flow down ahead of me in a rapid staccato of footsteps. _Must be the sword footwork. Yes, definitely trousers next time._ 

We reached the Iron Steed and took out seats, the ASI men ready to ride. Travis took out his deck of cards and started shuffling them one handed. Little green sparks shot between the cards, when Travis took one and slapped it on the side of Wraithbreaker sitting in the mount. A web of tiny green lightning washed over it and the Steed, leaving and almost indiscernible greenish glow. 

"You have about an hour before it dissipates. Godspeed," he said. 

Decker kicked at the machine and it made no sound as it started vibrating. We had talked about silencing the gun last night, but Travis went a step further and silenced that too. We rode down the mostly empty streets. For now, we had time and soon enough we were at the base of the house. We were really lucky, no one was watching. 

"Can you see any of the other two?" McCoy asked. 

I couldn't. "No, just this one." 

"Right, I can deal with that." He nodded to Tom. 

"Cans on, boys," Tom ordered and the agents took out these long, cylindrical objects and started twisting them onto the ends of their rifles. I guessed these were the enquieteners. "We're ready." 

I took aim at the creature. The angle was awkward, but not impossible. 

I pulled the trigger and Wraithbreaker bucked in the brace with the tiniest "p'too" in history. The contrast was uncanny.

The shot connected and the creature hit the roof, then rolled down and splattered disgustingly on the cobbles. Immediately followed by a flurry of claps and bullets tearing through the creature. It dissipated into the air, leaving only a small fortune in precious metals on the scuffed street. 

"Let's go!" Decker ordered and we moved a few streets, then stopped. 

"Decker, I can't see any!" I told him. "Maybe we should go back to the church?" 

"No need. Get out, we're moving up. Grab my arm and close your eyes," he ordered.

I got out as asked and extended a hand onto his shoulder. 

He started one of his little dances, golden sparkles appearing. The sound of filling sails reached me the same moment I felt myself be yanked somewhere by my soul. My vision shifted painfully into vertigo as I saw golden sparkles and some kind of forest zipping past at unholy speeds. 

_I should have closed my eyes._ 

I bent over, uneasy, trying to keep my balance on the angled floor, leaning heavily on Decker. I blinked. I wasn't on an angled floor. I was on a roof. 

I had no time to waste, so I straightened myself and searched for the monsters. There were six of them, all floating in one direction, over and through buildings. "I see six, all going... roughly west!" I pointed in the general direction. 

"Are you sure? I don't have that many of those in me." He made a general motion between us and the Steed, waiting in the street. 

I looked around again, but found no change. Having killed the nearest creature put us to the side of the "corridor" they were taking toward their destination. The tension was building, we were in pursuit of creatures that treated difficult terrain and obstructions like suggestions. I was on high alert, Witnessing and my hands shook a bit from my blood running hot.

"As much as I can be, I think," I said. 

"Good enough, we're going down. Ready?" he asked. 

I braced myself and put my hand on his shoulder again, this time closing my eyes tightly. "Yes." 

After a few of Decker's shoulder movements that feeling came again. Like being dragged by my soul somewhere else. It was much less uncomfortable with my eyes closed. We were on the street again, next to the Steed. 

We drove west, in the direction the creatures were going. A silent machine, ghostlike, followed by hoofbeats on stone. I tracked the creatures over roofs and down streets until we could set up to shoot. We had to outrun both the creatures and the people wandering the streets earliest. 

Then load, aim, fire. It was never a hard shot. Pretty close in, big target. I still missed the third one the first time, because it was moving sideways rather than toward us. We managed to kill four before we left the city behind us and the landscape opened. This side of Tucson it was relatively flat, with good roads and plenty of distance from people, right until the hills and mesas beyond. 

The silencing spell was no longer critical, but still welcome. Maintaining Witness was an uncomfortable strain on my senses, so I was glad of the reduction in noise at least. I still had to deal with the world shaking whenever we hit bumps in the roads, now dirt instead of cobblestone. 

We set up ahead and waited on the last two to approach. Soon both emerged from the city, just the two, though noticeably higher than I expected them to be. I could still hit them if I aimed right. We moved to intercept, while I watched for any I missed earlier. There were no more. 

"Decker, they were going down through a cliff from the laboratory. Why are they going up all of a sudden?" I asked, while watching the monsters slowly approach from the distance. 

"They are? Hmmm... By your best estimate, where are they going now?" 

I looked back and forth a few times and pointed to a distant rock formation. "Toward that mountain thing there. I don't think they're going for the top?" 

"I think... I think they were all going there. Look there, beyond Tucson, where the woods go up the hill. That's where the laboratory is. I'd say it roughly matches. And I know where they're going now. We'll still leave one, just in case I'm wrong." 

By this point we could detect and eliminate the creatures easily, especially that we had time to prepare. We left the last one intact and followed it right until it prepared to go into and through the rising terrain.

___B'TOOM!___
